# Newton's cannon

In *A Treatise of the System of the World* (1728) Newton imagines a cannon on top of a very high mountain firing horizontally, harder and harder. The projectile lands farther and farther away, until it goes round the Earth and never lands again. The app repeats the thought experiment with real gravity: |F| = G M m / r², computed step by step.

## How to use it

1. Start from the three questions in the “Before firing” box and have students write down their predictions.
2. Choose the speed with the slider or with the 2, 4, 6, 7, **circle**, 9, **escape** buttons and press **Fire!**. The paths stay on the screen, each in its own colour, as in Newton's figure; below you see the distance along the ground and the flight time.
3. Compare 2 and 4 km/s: the distance does not double, because the ground curves away under the projectile.
4. With **circle** the projectile goes round the Earth and comes back to V: the force |F⟩ is always perpendicular to the velocity |v⟩. At 9 km/s the orbit is an ellipse and the angle changes along the way. With **escape** it never comes back.
5. Change the height of the mountain: the speed for a circular orbit √(G M / r) and the escape speed √(2 G M / r) decrease with altitude.

“Fast forward” speeds up the animation; “Clear the paths” starts again.

## Good to know

- Data: Earth radius 6371 km, G M = 3.986·10¹⁴ m³/s² (|g| = 9.8 m/s² at the surface). No air, and the Earth does not rotate.
- The mountain is very high (400 km at the start, from 50 to 3000 km), as in Newton's figure, so that the projectile flies above the atmosphere.
- From a 400 km summit: circular orbit at 7.67 km/s with a period of about 92 minutes, like the Space Station.
- Newton's original figure (1728) is in the public domain and is reproduced in the app.
