# A map of the Riemann proof

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge announced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis written in a language that a computer can check line by line. This page follows the route of the proof in seven stages, explained with high-school maths.

## How to use it

1. Read “What it is about”: prime numbers, the law that counts them and how wrong it can be.
2. Look at the **prime seismograph**: the graph is computed in your browser with all the primes up to one million. If the hypothesis is true, the curve never leaves the dashed band.
3. Go through the seven stages. The parts with formulas are in boxes you can open, for those who want to go deeper.

## Warning

Two independent programs have accepted the proof, but at the time of publication no mathematician had yet read and approved it in full. The page follows the reading map of the repository [nasqret/riemann-rev](https://github.com/nasqret/riemann-rev), which is not the proof and does not certify it.
