# From polygon to circle

A ball moves straight along the sides of a regular polygon and gets a kick at every vertex. As the number of sides grows, the polygon becomes the circle: you can see why the force always points to the centre and becomes perpendicular to the velocity.

## How to use it

1. Start from the triangle (3 sides). Before showing the kicks, ask the class where the kick at the vertex must point.
2. Look at the **Velocity triangle** panel: the two velocities start from the same point and the change |Δv⟩ = |v₂⟩ − |v₁⟩ lies on the dashed red line, the direction towards the centre. So the kick points to the centre.
3. Use the buttons 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, 36 and then **circle**. At each step press **Write in the table**: the angles between force and velocity are 90° ± 180°/n and tend to 90°.
4. With **Go to the next vertex** you move one kick at a time; the speed slider slows the animation down.
5. Finish with the row “average force · r / (m v²)”: it is 1 for any number of sides, so the average force is m v²/r.

## Good to know

On the polygon the kick points to the centre but is **not** perpendicular to the velocity: in the triangle it makes 150° with the velocity before and 30° with the velocity after. It becomes perpendicular only in the limit, on the circle. The sequence is designed so that students see the angles approach 90°.

Vectors are written as kets, as in our Year 3 classes: |v⟩ is the velocity vector, |v| its magnitude.
