# The golf ball in oil

A golf ball is dropped into a graduated cylinder full of seed oil. The video (4 October 2013, 30 frames per second) was analysed with Tracker: the app shows it in three steps, from the opening question to the graphs of position, velocity and acceleration.

## How to use it

1. Open the app on the projector and start from the **1 · The question** tab. Show the video without saying anything and ask how the ball moves: does it speed up, slow down or move at constant speed?
2. Go to **2 · The markers**. The red trail has one dot every 0.1 s: where the dots are equally spaced, the speed is constant. Use the ◀︎ 1 and 1 ▶︎ buttons to move back and forward by one frame.
3. Finish with **3 · The graphs**: position, velocity and acceleration against time, in sync with the video. Open “The numbers from the video analysis” for the measured terminal velocity (0.117 m/s) and the comparison with the models.

Keyboard: space plays and pauses, comma and full stop move by one frame. The ¼×, ½× and 1× buttons change the speed of the video.

You can open a tab directly by adding `#domanda`, `#marcatori` or `#grafici` to the address.

## What you see

- In the first 0.2 s the ball enters the oil at about 0.22 m/s, faster than its terminal velocity, and slows down.
- For a moment it drops below the terminal velocity (about 0.09 m/s), then from 0.6 s onwards it moves at constant speed.
- Neither Stokes' law (2.9 m/s) nor quadratic drag (0.52 m/s) predicts the measured speed: the ball is almost as wide as the cylinder, and the oil has to flow back up through the narrow gap between the ball and the glass.
